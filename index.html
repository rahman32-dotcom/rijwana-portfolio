Phase 3 — Comprehensive Cybersecurity Program. Scaled the plan into an executive-level program for a 150,000-customer regulated bank, aligned to NIST CSF, ISO/IEC 27001, the FFIEC Cybersecurity Assessment Tool, GLBA, and PCI-DSS. Set SMART targets — a 35% annual reduction in incidents, org-wide MFA by Q4, and cutting the patch cycle from 18 days to 72 hours — designed the governance structure (CISO, SOC Manager, Compliance Officer), built a risk-based budget (40% infrastructure, 20% risk/compliance audits, 15% training, 15% incident response, 10% continuity), and delivered a full policy set, SOPs, and process flowcharts (risk management lifecycle, six-phase incident response, access control workflow) as appendices.

Network Segmentation & VLAN Security
Independent research paper · Network security architecture
›

Traced network segmentation's evolution from flat, single-broadcast-domain networks through costly physical segmentation to VLANs — logical segmentation at Layer 2 under the IEEE 802.1Q tagging standard — and argued for why segmentation remains a cornerstone defense against lateral movement.

Worked through a concrete enterprise design (VLAN 10 HR, VLAN 20 Finance, VLAN 30 IT, VLAN 99 Management) layered with ACLs, firewalls, and IDS/IPS, then grounded it in industry practice: hospitals separating patient records, medical devices, and guest Wi-Fi for HIPAA; retailers isolating payment systems for PCI-DSS.

Covered the real failure modes — misconfiguration, and VLAN hopping via double tagging or switch spoofing — with the corresponding hardening steps (secure trunks, disabling unused ports, quarterly configuration audits), then traced where the field is headed: micro-segmentation, Zero Trust's "never trust, always verify" model, SDN-driven dynamic policy, and AI/ML systems that isolate a compromised device into a restricted VLAN automatically.

Cryptography — Foundations, Systems & Vulnerabilities
Three-part research series · Classical ciphers through post-quantum risk
›

Started from first principles — the Caesar cipher's substitution logic and how frequency analysis breaks it — to build up to why modern cryptography needs both a strong algorithm and a well-protected key.

Compared the three systems in production use today: symmetric encryption (AES, DES, 3DES — fast, but key distribution scales at n(n−1)/2 for n users), asymmetric encryption (RSA, ECC, Diffie-Hellman — solves distribution and enables digital signatures, at the cost of speed and larger keys), and hybrid systems that use asymmetric exchange for a symmetric session key, the pattern behind HTTPS and TLS. Underneath it all: the Euclidean and Extended Euclidean algorithms, Euler's phi function, and Fermat's/Euler's theorems that make RSA key generation possible, and the key-length trade-off where a 128-bit symmetric key matches roughly a 3072-bit RSA key.

Then catalogued where cryptography actually breaks in practice: deprecated algorithms (DES's short key length, MD5/SHA-1 collisions), implementation flaws (side-channel timing/power leakage, padding oracle attacks, insecure ECB-mode usage), key-management failures (weak random number generators, hardcoded or plaintext key storage, missed rotation), protocol-level incidents like Heartbleed and POODLE, and the looming threat of Shor's algorithm on quantum hardware driving the push toward post-quantum cryptography.
